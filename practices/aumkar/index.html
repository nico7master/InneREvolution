In Depth — For the Curious

Why This Sound?

Without the use of your tongue, your voice can produce only three fundamental sounds: “Aaa,” “Uuu,” and “Mmm.” Every word in every language is a permutation of these three plus the tongue's movements. Sounded together, the three emerge as AUM — which is why the yogic tradition holds it as the fundamental sound of physical existence itself.

This is not presented as faith but as observation of vibration: every sound has a form attached to it, and every form has a sound attached to it. Where there is a vibration, there is bound to be a sound. The reverberations of sound, in the yogic account of creation, consolidated into physical form — making sound the subtlest layer of physicality, the bridge between the material and the beyond.

What Chanting Does

“Just repetition of a mantra changes the chemistry of the system, because ultimately everything is just vibration and sound; the whole existence is just that. If you use the right sound properly, with the right amount of intensity and perseverance, it will change the chemistry of your system.”

The “Aaa” component alone has a distinct physiological role. It is the one reverberation that spreads right across the body, because it works on the Manipuraka — the center just below the navel where the 72,000 nadis meet and redistribute themselves. Activating this center is associated with health, dynamism and wellbeing.

Mantra, Yantra, Tantra

Chanting sits inside a precise science. Mantra is pure sound. Yantra is its corresponding form. Tantra is the technology of using these two dimensions together. Nada Yoga is the wider science of using sound to become one with existence.

A mantra, in this framework, is not emotional solace — it is a small piece of sound which, used with the right awareness, can open up a completely new dimension of life.

One honest note that the tradition itself insists on: without the necessary awareness, repeated sounds can deaden the mind rather than sharpen it. Mantra is a powerful instrument, and like any powerful instrument it needs to be used with attention — which is why learning it in its proper form, from a teacher, matters.
