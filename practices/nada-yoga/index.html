In Depth — For the Curious

The World as Sound

We experience the world as forms — objects, people, surfaces. But modern physics agrees with the yogis that there is no such thing as static matter: the universe is a web of energetic reverberations. And wherever there is vibration, there is sound. Nada Brahma means just this: experiencing the whole creation as sound, not as form.

“Yoga has always said the whole of existence is sound... Nada Yoga is the science of using sound to become one with existence.”

Sadhguru describes his own experience at Kanti Sarovar in the Himalayas: the surroundings gradually lost their form and only nada — sound — existed. The mountain, the lake, even his own body did not exist in their usual form. Everything was just sound. From that state, the Nada Brahma chant flowed out — a song about sound as the source of all life, karma, bondage and liberation.

The Four Dimensions of Sound

Nada Yoga maps sound as having four progressively subtler dimensions:

  • The spoken sound — the outermost layer, what the voice produces and the ear hears
  • The mental sound — the vibration of thought, the word produced by the mind
  • The deep-recess sound — the mind's ability to create sound from some deep recess, without any external input
  • Para Vak — “the voice of the Creator”: the sound of the utterly still, soundless consciousness, the source of creation

The teaching is direct: unless you transcend the first three levels and come to hear the beginningless, uncreated sound — the basis of the Creator — you will never know absolute spiritual fulfillment.

The Unstruck Sound: Anahata

Under normal physical conditions, sound requires friction — you strike two objects together. The un-struck sound is called Anahata — and it is also the name of the heart chakra, sitting exactly in the middle of the seven major chakras.

Anahata is described as the transition between your lower chakras and higher chakras — between survival instincts and the instinct to liberate yourself. A seeker who stabilizes their energy in this center evolves beyond circumstantial and environmental limitations.

Why Sound Works

Sound occupies a unique position in the yogic science: it is still physical, but it is the subtlest form of physicality. That makes it an ideal preparation for transcendence — it can create the right ambience. But the spiritual opens up only when you transcend from sound to silence.

This is why consecrated sounds — chants, mantras, classical music built on exact sound arrangements — are used in the tradition not as entertainment but as tools: they break physical and psychological limitations, open the system, and make it receptive. When Sadhguru consecrated the Dhyanalinga, the offering of sound (Nadha Aradhana) was built into its very functioning.
