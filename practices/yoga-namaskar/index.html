In Depth — For the Curious

Not Just an Exercise

The spine is not merely a collection of bones. As Sadhguru teaches in Karma, it is the very basis of communication and perception within the human system. Every life experience you have ever had was conducted through your spinal column — which is why keeping it in optimal condition will not just revitalize the body, but make a phenomenal difference in your mental and emotional life.

“The spine is not merely a collection of bones. It is the very basis of communication and perception within the human system. Keeping it in optimal condition will not just revitalize and rejuvenate the body but will make a phenomenal difference in your mental and emotional life and the way you function.”

The practice works with one central mechanism: the extension of the spine, particularly the lumbar region, has a profound impact on psychological wellbeing. That is the whole logic — a stretched, relaxed spinal column is not a physical concern alone; it is a direct lever on how you feel and function.

What It Does

Yoga Namaskar is a powerful process of activating and strengthening the spine and the muscles alongside it, so that as one ages, the spinal system does not collapse and the nerves are not pinched. If there is already spinal damage, it is a good way to regenerate the spine and ensure all-around benefits for the entire body.

But its most distinctive application is emotional. The practice is described as a scientific approach to unblocking emotional impasses in the human system — promoting what the classical texts call chitta vritti nirodha: the liberation of consciousness from fluctuations and bottlenecks.

Seven steps, a few minutes, no equipment — and unlike Surya Namaskar's twelve postures, its seven movements work the spine through its full range in a form anyone can follow.
