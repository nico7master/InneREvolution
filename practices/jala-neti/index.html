In Depth — For the Curious

Why the Sinuses Matter So Much

Most people think of the sinuses as a minor corner of the anatomy — until they block. Sadhguru describes them very differently:

“Your sinuses are a very complex plumbing job. Where they are blocked and where they are leaking depends on a variety of factors... How clear your sinuses are and how well-balanced the fluids are especially in the head region determines a variety of things, including your brain function, your sense of wellbeing, your sense of balance, the sharpness of your intellect, and the sharpness of the five senses.”

Brain function. Wellbeing. Balance. The sharpness of the intellect and of all five senses — all of it riding on how clear and well-balanced this region is. When the sinuses congest, it is not just a stuffy nose; it dulls the whole instrument of perception.

What Jala Neti Does

The process cleanses the nasal passages with salt water, removing excess mucus and pollutants from the nasal passages and sinuses. The result, in Sadhguru's framework, is preparatory: reducing the mucus in the system is a first step toward keeping the sinuses well-balanced and maintaining free movement within them — which the deeper practices then build upon.

“If you are effectively doing kapalabhati for a period of time, it will establish this balance. If you want a preparatory step, you could do jala neti to reduce the mucus in the system. But this practice has to be properly imparted. Our Hatha Yoga teachers would be able to teach you if needed.”

It also stands against a common modern habit. Sadhguru warns against chemically drying up the sinus fluids with tablets — these fluids are needed, and the proper functioning of the five senses depends on how the fluid movement happens. Jala Neti works with the body's own mechanics instead of overriding them.

Why Learning It Properly Matters

This is a practice where the how matters as much as the what — and that is exactly why it is not self-taught from a description. The practice has to be properly imparted by a trained Hatha Yoga teacher. Learned correctly, it is simple, gentle, and takes only a few minutes — and many practitioners make it a permanent part of their morning routine, like brushing the teeth, but for the breath.
